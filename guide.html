Chess Repertoire: your opening trainer

A free app for your PC that helps you build the openings you want to play, and practise them until you know them by heart.

  • Build your lines on a board, or bring them in from chess.com or Lichess.
  • Train: the app plays your opponent's moves and you find yours. Lines you get wrong come back sooner; lines you know come back less often, so your time goes where it's needed.
  • Learn from the greats: famous master games for every position, and the Stockfish engine to check your ideas.
The app https://tobiashk-git.github.io/chess-repertoire/
Part 1 · about 5 minutes · no account needed

Use it on your PC

1.

Install the app

Open the app link in Edge or Chrome. Click the install icon at the right end of the address bar, then Install. It gets its own window and a Start menu icon.

Always use the same browser on your PC. Your repertoire is saved in it.

2.

Build and train

You start with an empty board: your repertoire is yours to shape.

  1. White / Black at the top switches between your two repertoires.
  2. Play moves on the board, then press Save line. To add another variation, go back to where it branches and play a different move.
  3. Press Train and choose Review due (what needs practice today), Practise (every line, any time) or Real game (replies as often as masters play them).
3.

Not sure where to begin? (optional)

Load a short suggested repertoire and build from there:

⋯ (top right) › Start from a suggested repertoire

White
1.e4 Attacking (King's Gambit, Smith-Morra…), 1.e4 Classical (Italian Game…), 1.d4 London System, 1.d4 Queen's Gambit
Black
against 1.e4: Caro-Kann, 1…e5 or Sicilian Najdorf; against 1.d4: Queen's Gambit Declined or King's Indian

Each is only 6–8 moves deep, covers the main replies and has short notes on the plans. Change, extend or delete lines as you like.

Already know someone whose openings you like? ⋯ › Copy from someone's repertoire copies one of their openings, or all of them.

Part 2 · optional · about 10 minutes, once

Also train on your phone

Build on the PC, then send your repertoire to your phone and train on the go. This needs a free GitHub account, which is what carries your repertoire from the PC to the phone.

4.

Create a GitHub account

  1. Sign up for free at github.com/signup.
  2. Send Tobias your GitHub username.
  3. Tobias adds you to the app and you get an email invitation. Click Accept invitation, or open the invitations page.
5.

Make a publishing token

On github.com: profile picture › Settings › Developer settings › Personal access tokens › Tokens (classic) › Generate new token (classic)

Click Generate token and copy it straight away. GitHub shows it only once.

It has to be a classic token. GitHub's newer “fine-grained” tokens can't publish to an app you've been invited to.

6.

Publish from your PC

Press Publish master. Do it again whenever you want your phone to have your latest changes.

7.

Set up your phone

  1. Open the app link in Safari. If it opens inside WhatsApp, choose Open in Safari first. Then tap Share › Add to Home Screen and open the app from the new icon.
  2. ⋯ › Master copy › This device: Follows a master, choose your name, then tap Update in the yellow banner.
  3. From then on, after each publish the phone offers the update the next time you open it. Your training progress is kept.

Good to know

  • Back up now and then: ⋯ › Back up everything. It saves a file with your whole repertoire and training progress.
  • Using the phone too? Make your changes on the PC. An update replaces changes made on the phone, unless you tap Send my changes first in the banner and import that file on the PC.
  • Publishing says the token was “not accepted”? It has probably expired. Make a new classic token (step 5) and paste it in again.
  • Published repertoires are public. Anyone with the app's repository link can read them.
  • New feature missing? The bottom of the ⋯ panel shows the app version. Updates take about a minute to arrive, so wait and reload.
